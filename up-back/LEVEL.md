# Up Back

A wet November yard in East Tennessee, built by hand from photographs. Open `up-back/index.html`.

It starts in the den (one wall, no roof, rain on the carpet) and climbs through oak leaves to the Airstream. Ten things to find, shown one at a time. Past the Airstream a trough gate leads into the trough (`atlas/JERRYCAN.md`).

## Playing it

- **On a phone:** the left thumb is a stick, the right thumb turns the view, **Fire** shoots paint, and the button above it does what is here (get up, drive, get out, pick a fig).
- **On a keyboard:** W A S D, arrows turn, Shift runs, E does what is here, Space or F shoots.

## Everything with wheels or tracks runs

| Vehicle | How it drives |
|---|---|
| The Deere track loader | spins where it stands; slow; ploughs the leaves and counts them |
| The zero-turn mower | spins where it stands; quicker; you sit on it |
| The black Denali | steers like a truck; fast; heavy to pull away |
| The Suburban | the same, slower, and it smokes |
| The red bicycle (behind the camper) | steers and leans; no reverse |

Each sits on the ground under its own length and width, squats when it pulls away, rolls into a turn, and bumps when it hits something solid. Vehicles block each other and the walker.

## The excavator, buckets, digging, breaking

- **The excavator** stands at the top of the yard, past the Airstream to the west: tracks nearly seven metres long, a house that swings, a boom, a stick and a toothed bucket. It is slow and nothing stops it: its tracks and bucket break whatever they meet.
- **Arm mode** (the yellow button, or R): the tracks stop and both thumbs work the arm.
  - Excavator: left thumb swings (left/right) and reaches (up/down); right thumb lifts the boom (up/down) and curls the bucket (left/right). On a keyboard: W A S D and the arrows.
  - Deere: right thumb lifts the arms and tips the bucket.
- **Digging.** Put the teeth in the ground and curl in: the ground goes down a square metre at a time, up to six metres deep, and the bucket fills (5 m³ on the excavator). Swing, curl out, and it tips into a pile. The Deere scrapes a little when driven forward with the bucket down. Everything in the world, people, vehicles, leaves, props, follows the new ground.
- **Picking up.** A bucket that closes on loose things carries them: cans, tyres, drums, the cart, and the pieces of anything broken. Curl out and they are thrown with the bucket's speed.
- **Breaking.** Everything placed in the yard comes apart into its own boards, panels and parts, each with a body: the den, the camper, the Airstream, the trailer, the garage panel by panel (lose three and the roof comes down), the bench and engine inside it. Trees fall the way they are pushed. The trucks and the Deere have to ram at speed; the mower and bicycle cannot.
- **Going in.** The garage is open to the south: a bench with paint cans, an engine on a stand, tyres, one bulb. The Airstream has a room in it (Step inside, once you have laid a hand on it). The den was always open.

## Uncle Chip, the donkey and the coyote

- **Uncle Chip** is the one in the chair, after the MESA CHIP film: a lean, fair, weathered man; a tan hat with a creased crown and a wide brim rolled a little at the sides; round wire glasses; hair to the shoulders; a full brown-grey beard down the chest, ragged at the end, with a heavy moustache; a white shirt, jeans, and boots that are half sandal. The hat, hair, beard and glasses are sculpted in `up-back.js` and ride his head bone. The cast paints faces from portraits, so his skin is re-toned in the shader, keeping the portrait's light and dark. `?nostart` plus `window.__ubCam = { pos, at, fov }` gives a fixed camera for close study.
- **The donkey** grazes by the fig bush, drinks at the creek, and backs away from moving machines. With a fig in your pocket it comes to you; give it one and it follows for a minute and a half. It can be ridden (W walks on, A and D turn, Shift trots). It brays at the coyote.
- **The coyote** keeps to the edge of the woods, bolts from people, engines and paint, howls now and then, and comes in for ripe figs on the bushes you planted when nobody is near.
- Paint sticks to both.

## What happens

- **Driving.** A vehicle has a heading and, separately, a velocity. Tyres pull one round to the other only as fast as their grip allows: full on dry ground, less on loose fill, very little in water. So the trucks slide and the tracked machines do not. A vehicle leaves the ground when the ground falls away under a climb (a pile is a ramp), comes down under gravity, and lands with a thump. It presses ruts into wet or loose earth and throws spray in water.
- **Water.** It is raining, so any hole deeper than about 30 cm slowly fills. Running water carries loose fill off with it: a dam of dumped earth holds until it is overtopped, then breaches.
- **Earth.** Take the ground from under a tree or a structure (about 90 cm) and it comes down.
- **What the yard kept.** Seven things are buried. On foot, a tick in the ear quickens as you walk over one; dig to its depth and it comes up out of the ground.
- Two more jobs: dig up three things the yard kept, and get a vehicle off the ground for most of a second.

## Groundwork

Our own take on the earthmoving sandbox, sized for a phone.

- **Soil in layers.** Topsoil for the first half metre digs easily and shows dark. Clay, to two metres, digs at half the rate and shows orange. Gravel below is slow, grey, and only the excavator cuts it. Loose fill is paler.
- **Slumping.** Nothing stands steeper than its material allows: piles spread to about 37 degrees and cut banks fall in past about 52.
- **Water.** A spring in the west woods feeds a creek that runs down the side of the yard. Water moves a square metre at a time, downhill, pools in any hole, and soaks away slowly. Dig a ditch from the creek and it follows. Wheels bog in it; tracks wade. A fig with water at its roots fruits again, and anything planted near water grows three and a half times as fast.
- **Grade stakes.** On foot, **Stake** (or G) plants one end of a line, then the other. A strip five metres wide between them shows red where the ground is high, blue where it is low, green where it is on grade, with the share on grade counted. On the Deere, **Grade** holds the bucket to the line: drive it and the bucket cuts the red, carries it, and fills the blue.
- **The dump truck** stands near the excavator. Tip a bucket over its bed to load it (8 m³). **Tip** raises the bed and pours a pile behind.
- **One-tap digging.** In the excavator, **Scoop** runs a whole stroke (lower, curl in, lift) and **Dump** curls out. Either thumb on a stick takes the arm back.
- **Four jobs**, each measured from the ground and the water themselves and shown with a running figure: dig a ditch from the creek to the fig bush; stake and grade a line of 15 m or more to 85%; haul 8 m³ to the pad with the cones; make a pond of 10 m³ in a hole of your own.
- **The map** also shows water, the grade line and the pad.

## A place with things to do

Fifteen things, shown one at a time, each with an orange arrow and a distance on screen: get up, the photograph, the mower, knock down six cans, the Denali, pick a fig, the generator, the Suburban, the Deere, plant a fig, the ruin, the camper, the trailer, the excavator, the Airstream.

- **The ruin of a house** stands east of the yard in the woods: a stone foundation a few courses high with gaps, a chimney, a door frame, a rusted bed frame, steps, and a cellar hole. Every length of wall breaks on its own; the chimney comes down from the bottom.
- **Growing.** Pick a fig, dig a hole with either bucket, stand in it and plant. The fig comes up in about a minute and a half and bears four figs, which can be planted in turn.
- **Making things work.** The generator in the garage starts on the cord: the bulb comes up and so does a string of lights down to the den. Break the generator and they go out.
- **The map** (the Map button, or M): the yard from above, north up, with every painted square metre in its colour, holes and piles, what is standing and what is broken, trees, vehicles, plants, you and your friend. It gives square metres painted and the share of up back.
- **Paint sticks to everything it hits.** On the ground and walls it is the leaded glass. On vehicles, people and loose things it is wet discs fixed to them, so it rides with them. Trees are the exception (see Not yet).

## Physics (Rapier)

The ground, trees and walls are solid. Loose things have real bodies and tumble: the yellow cart, the white barrel, two pyramids of paint cans, four tyres, two drums. Vehicles and people shove them; paintballs knock them and count them.

## Paint

The gun is MESA CHIP's (`mesa/ink.js`): a ball on an arc that lands wet, runs on walls, and cures into leaded stained glass. The ground, the den wall and the garage take paint. The header counts square metres of glass.

## For two (`mesa/net.js`, words in `duo.js`)

"Play with a friend" on the first card makes a link (`…/up-back/?with=cobalt-horn-35`) and opens the share sheet. Whoever opens it is in the yard too, in an olive coat.

- Each phone moves its own player and resolves hits on itself: a friend's ball that lands on you, or on the vehicle you are driving, is a tag. The header shows tags for and against.
- Whoever last drove a vehicle owns its position; the other phone follows it. A vehicle someone is driving cannot be taken.
- Shared as well: figs planted, the generator, the arm positions, everything broken, every tree felled, and every bucketful dug or tipped.
- Not shared: loose props and pieces, what a bucket is carrying, leaves, figs, and paint already on the ground when someone joins (new paint is, because every shot is replayed on both phones).
- Peer to peer through PeerJS's public broker; no account and no server of ours. Tested with two tabs over a stand-in channel (`?netlocal`), not yet phone to phone.
